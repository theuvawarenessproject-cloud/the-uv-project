Start with the basics

Get to know the UV.

Ultraviolet (UV) rays are a form of energy from the sun and some artificial sources, such as tanning beds. Too much UV exposure can damage skin cells and increase the risk of skin cancer.

01 / UNDERSTAND

What is skin cancer?

Skin cancer happens when skin cells grow in an uncontrolled way. There are different types, and noticing changes early is important.

02 / REMEMBER

UV is year-round.

UV rays can reach you on cloudy or cool days, and can reflect off surfaces like water, sand, and pavement.

03 / KNOW

Everyone needs protection.

People of every skin tone can experience UV damage and develop skin cancer. Sun protection is for everyone.

Check before you head out

Know your UV Index.

The UV Index tells you how strong the sun’s UV radiation is expected to be. When it is 3 or higher, take steps to protect your skin.

Everyday sun safety

Make protection a habit.

There is no single way to protect yourself. Combining a few simple habits gives you better protection when you spend time outdoors.

☂

Find some shade

Use shade from trees, umbrellas, or covered areas, especially when UV levels are high.

◉

Choose sunscreen

Use broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher on exposed skin. Reapply about every two hours and after swimming or sweating.

▤

Cover up

Wear protective clothing, a wide-brimmed hat, and sunglasses that block UVA and UVB rays.

✕

Skip indoor tanning

Tanning beds expose skin to UV radiation. Avoid indoor tanning and choose sun-safe ways to enjoy the outdoors.

Know what to look for

The ABCDEs of melanoma.

Changes in a mole or spot can be worth discussing with a healthcare professional. The ABCDE guide can help you remember some warning signs.

A

Asymmetry

One half looks different from the other half.

B

Border

Edges look irregular, uneven, or poorly defined.

C

Color

Color varies across the spot.

D

Diameter

The spot may be larger than 6 mm, though melanomas can be smaller.

E

Evolving

The spot changes in size, shape, color, or another noticeable way.

This guide is for awareness, not diagnosis. Not every skin cancer follows the ABCDE pattern. If you notice a new, changing, unusual, or non-healing spot, tell a trusted adult and ask a healthcare professional to check it.
